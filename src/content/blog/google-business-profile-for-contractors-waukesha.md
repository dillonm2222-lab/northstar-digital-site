When someone in Waukesha needs a plumber, a roofer, or a masonry contractor, the first thing they do is pull out their phone and search Google. What shows up next determines who gets the call.

Those three businesses you see at the top of Google Maps with stars and reviews? They're not better than everyone else. They're just better at showing up.

Here's how to get your contracting business into that map pack — step by step.

## What Is a Google Business Profile?

A Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) is the free listing that shows your business on Google Maps and in the "local pack" that appears at the top of search results. It shows your business name, phone number, hours, address, reviews, and photos.

If you don't have one, or if yours is incomplete, you are invisible to the majority of people searching for your service right now.

## Step 1: Claim or Create Your Profile

Go to [business.google.com](https://business.google.com) and search for your business name. If it already exists (Google sometimes creates auto-generated listings), claim it. If it doesn't, create a new one.

You'll need to verify your business. Google usually does this by mailing a postcard to your business address with a verification code. This takes 5–10 days.

**Tip:** Use your actual business address — not a P.O. box. Google requires a physical location for most service businesses. If you work from home, you can hide the address but still list your service area.

## Step 2: Fill Out Every Field — Every Single One

Incomplete profiles rank lower. Google rewards businesses that give it more information to work with. Here's what to fill out:

- **Business name:** Use your exact legal name. Don't stuff keywords in here (Google will suspend you).
- **Category:** Pick the most specific primary category you can. "Masonry Contractor" beats "Contractor." Add secondary categories too.
- **Phone number:** Use a local number. (262) area codes outperform 800 numbers for local searches.
- **Website:** Link to your actual website. If you don't have one, this is the single biggest reason to get one.
- **Hours:** Be accurate — wrong hours hurt your ranking and frustrate customers.
- **Service area:** List every city and town you serve. Waukesha, Brookfield, Pewaukee, Oconomowoc, Menomonee Falls — anywhere you'll actually drive to.
- **Services:** List every specific service you offer. "Brick patio installation," "concrete driveway repair," "tuckpointing," etc. Be specific.
- **Description:** Write 2–3 sentences about your business. Include your main service and your location naturally. Example: "GM Masonry Concrete provides custom masonry, brick, and concrete work for homeowners and businesses in Waukesha County. Licensed and insured, with over X years of experience."

## Step 3: Add Photos — Real Ones

Listings with photos get significantly more engagement than those without. But not just any photos. Google (and customers) want to see:

- **Your work:** Before and after shots, completed projects, jobs in progress
- **Your team:** Photos of you on a job site build trust immediately
- **Your truck/equipment:** Shows you're a real, established operation
- **Your logo:** Makes your listing look professional in map results

Add at least 10 photos when you first set up your profile, and keep adding more as you complete jobs. Ask customers if you can photograph the finished work.

## Step 4: Get Reviews — Systematically

Reviews are the single biggest ranking factor in local Google Maps results. Five reviews beats zero reviews. Fifty reviews beats five.

The problem is most customers don't leave reviews unless you ask. Here's a simple system:

1. At the end of every job, say: "Hey, we'd really appreciate if you could leave us a Google review — it helps a lot. I can send you a direct link to make it easy."
2. Text or email the customer your Google review link (you can get this from your GBP dashboard under "Get more reviews").
3. Follow up once if they don't leave a review within a week.

Don't fake reviews or ask friends and family who aren't real customers. Google will remove them and may penalize your profile.

## Step 5: Keep It Active

A dormant profile ranks lower than an active one. Here's what "active" looks like:

- **Post updates:** Google lets you post updates, offers, and events to your profile. Post something once a week — a completed job photo, a seasonal offer, a tip for homeowners.
- **Respond to reviews:** Reply to every review, positive or negative. Keep it professional. Thank reviewers by name. For negative reviews, acknowledge the issue and offer to make it right.
- **Answer questions:** People can ask questions directly on your GBP. Answer them promptly.
- **Update your hours for holidays:** Missing this causes customer frustration and hurts your ranking.

## What If You're Doing All This and Still Not Showing Up?

If your profile is complete, you have reviews, and you're still not ranking in the top 3 for your area, the issue is usually one of:

1. **Your website** — Google ranks local businesses higher when their website is also optimized for local SEO. A weak or missing website is the most common reason a well-set-up GBP still doesn't rank.
2. **Competition** — Certain trades in certain areas are just more competitive. The answer is more reviews and more consistent content.
3. **Distance** — Google prioritizes proximity. If you're in Waukesha but most searches are coming from Brookfield, you may rank lower there even with a perfect profile.

## The Bottom Line

A fully optimized Google Business Profile is the single fastest way to start showing up when local customers search for your service. It's free, it works, and most of your competitors aren't doing it right.

The catch: it takes consistent effort to maintain, and the website piece is what most contractors skip — which is exactly why it matters.
