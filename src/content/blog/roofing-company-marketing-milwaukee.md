Roofing in Wisconsin is a storm-driven business. When hail hits Milwaukee or a wind event rolls through Waukesha County, your phone should be ringing. If it's not — or if you're missing calls while you're on jobs — you're leaving significant money behind.

Here's the marketing system that makes sure you capture every lead when storms drive demand.

## The Problem: Storm Season Creates Demand Spikes You're Not Capturing

After a major storm event in the Milwaukee area, homeowners are actively searching for roofers within hours. They're not waiting. They're Googling, calling, and booking the first contractor who picks up or responds.

If your Google Business Profile isn't showing up, if your website doesn't load fast on mobile, or if you're missing calls while you're on a job — those leads go to competitors. And in roofing, each job is worth thousands.

## Google Business Profile: Your #1 Storm Season Tool

When someone searches "roofer Milwaukee" or "roof damage repair Waukesha" after a storm, the Google map pack is where most people click. Getting into those three spots requires:

**Reviews — and a lot of them.** Milwaukee roofing is competitive. Companies with 50+ reviews dominate the map pack. Get there first and stay there. One good storm season where you collect 20 reviews can change your ranking for years.

**Storm-specific keywords in your profile.** Make sure your GBP description and services list mention storm damage, hail damage, insurance claims, and emergency tarping — these are the searches that spike after weather events.

**Photos of storm damage repairs.** Before/after photos of hail damage, missing shingles, and completed jobs build trust instantly and help your profile stand out.

## Missing Calls During a Storm Rush Is Costly

During peak demand after a storm, you and your crew are everywhere — on roofs, driving between inspections, on insurance calls. You will miss calls.

The difference between a contractor who captures those leads and one who loses them is **missed-call text-back**.

When you miss a call, the system texts the homeowner within 30 seconds:

> "Hey! Sorry I missed you — we're out on inspections right now. Can you tell me your address and what you're dealing with? I'll get back to you today. — [Your name], [Business Name]"

The homeowner texts back. You have their address, you know their issue, and you're in the conversation — even though you didn't pick up.

For a roofing company after a major storm event, this can mean the difference between capturing 30 leads and 15.

## Insurance Claims: Be the Contractor Who Makes It Easy

A big part of Milwaukee residential roofing is insurance work. Homeowners filing claims need a contractor who knows the process — public adjusters, supplement requests, Xactimate, the works.

Your website and GBP should clearly communicate that you handle insurance claims and make it easy for homeowners. A page or section titled "Storm Damage & Insurance Claims" targeting keywords like "insurance roof claim Milwaukee" or "hail damage roofer Waukesha" can be a consistent source of inbound leads.

## Reviews After Every Job

Every satisfied roofing customer is a potential review. And in a business where trust is everything — people are letting you onto their home — reviews from neighbors and locals carry serious weight.

After every job, send a text:

> "Hey [Name] — glad we got that taken care of for you before the next rain. If you have a quick second, a Google review would really help us out: [link]. Thanks for trusting us with your home."

Automate this and you'll collect reviews without thinking about it. Contractors who do this consistently end up dominating their local map pack within 6–12 months.

## Stay Visible Between Storms

The slow season for roofing (late fall, winter) is when your marketing should be working hardest — not resting. A few pieces of content targeting off-season searches can keep leads coming in:

- "How to tell if your roof needs replacing" (year-round search)
- "Roof inspection before winter Milwaukee"
- "How long does a roof last in Wisconsin weather"

These aren't storm-driven searches but they bring in homeowners planning ahead — often easier closes than emergency calls.
